# Research Workflow

## Step 1: Define the Research Question

Clearly identify:

* what needs to be researched,
* why the research is needed,
* what decision or outcome it will support,
* relevant constraints,
* and required freshness.

Avoid broad research when a focused question is sufficient.

## Step 2: Identify Research Areas

Break the question into relevant areas.

Possible areas include:

* technology,
* architecture,
* security,
* compatibility,
* performance,
* ecosystem,
* cost,
* maintenance,
* standards,
* and implementation requirements.

## Step 3: Gather Reliable Information

Prefer:

* official documentation,
* primary sources,
* recognized standards,
* authoritative technical sources,
* maintained project documentation,
* and reliable engineering resources.

Use community discussions as supplementary evidence when practical experience is relevant, while recognizing their limitations.

## Step 4: Verify Freshness

For time-sensitive technical information, verify:

* version,
* publication or update date,
* current support status,
* compatibility,
* and recent ecosystem changes.

Do not rely on outdated information when freshness materially affects the decision.

## Step 5: Compare Findings

Evaluate relevant options based on the user's actual scenario.

Consider:

* functionality,
* compatibility,
* security,
* performance,
* scalability,
* maintainability,
* learning curve,
* ecosystem,
* cost,
* and long-term sustainability.

## Step 6: Separate Facts and Recommendations

Clearly distinguish:

* verified facts,
* uncertain information,
* assumptions,
* trade-offs,
* and recommendations.

Do not present recommendations as objective facts.

## Step 7: Make a Practical Recommendation

Recommend the most suitable option for the user's specific requirements.

Explain:

* why it fits,
* important advantages,
* important limitations,
* alternatives considered,
* and situations where another option may be better.

## Step 8: Provide Actionable Next Steps

Translate the research into practical next actions when useful.

Do not provide unnecessary information unrelated to the user's decision.
