# Solve Problem Workflow

## Step 1: Understand the Problem

Identify:

* the actual problem,
* expected outcome,
* inputs,
* outputs,
* constraints,
* assumptions,
* and success criteria.

Ask for clarification when missing information materially affects the solution.

## Step 2: Break Down the Problem

Divide complex problems into smaller parts when useful.

Identify:

* dependencies,
* bottlenecks,
* important relationships,
* failure conditions,
* and relevant constraints.

## Step 3: Research if Needed

Research relevant:

* current approaches,
* standards,
* official guidance,
* algorithms,
* technologies,
* or domain knowledge

when research could materially improve the solution.

Avoid unnecessary research for stable and well-understood problems.

## Step 4: Identify Solution Approaches

Consider:

* the simplest correct solution,
* important alternative solutions,
* industry-standard approaches,
* and innovative approaches when they provide meaningful value.

Do not overwhelm the user with irrelevant possibilities.

## Step 5: Compare Approaches

Evaluate meaningful solutions based on relevant factors such as:

* correctness,
* complexity,
* time complexity,
* memory usage,
* maintainability,
* scalability,
* security,
* reliability,
* and implementation difficulty.

## Step 6: Recommend the Best Approach

Recommend the solution most appropriate for the user's actual scenario.

Clearly distinguish between:

* theoretically optimal,
* practically optimal,
* simplest,
* and industry-standard approaches when relevant.

Explain why the recommendation fits the scenario.

## Step 7: Verify the Reasoning

Before implementation:

* reconsider the logic,
* check assumptions,
* consider edge cases,
* check important constraints,
* and verify that the approach satisfies the requirements.

## Step 8: Implement or Explain

Implement the solution or provide the explanation according to the user's requested interaction style.

Keep the solution appropriate to the actual requirements.

## Step 9: Verify the Result

Check:

* correctness,
* important edge cases,
* integration where relevant,
* performance requirements,
* and relevant failure scenarios.

Do not claim verification unless it actually occurred.

## Step 10: Explain the Final Solution

Explain:

* the problem,
* important reasoning,
* approaches considered,
* the chosen solution,
* why it works,
* trade-offs,
* and limitations.
