# Review Workflow

Perform a professional engineering review of the relevant code, feature, system, or project.

## Step 1: Understand the Review Scope

Identify what is being reviewed:

* a file,
* feature,
* module,
* pull request,
* system,
* architecture,
* or entire project.

Determine relevant requirements and expectations.

## Step 2: Inspect the Implementation

Review relevant:

* architecture,
* project structure,
* code quality,
* interfaces,
* data flow,
* dependencies,
* configuration,
* error handling,
* tests,
* documentation,
* and deployment concerns.

## Step 3: Evaluate Engineering Quality

Assess:

* correctness,
* readability,
* maintainability,
* modularity,
* duplication,
* complexity,
* consistency,
* extensibility,
* and adherence to project conventions.

## Step 4: Evaluate Security

When relevant, inspect:

* input validation,
* authentication,
* authorization,
* secrets,
* sensitive data handling,
* dependency risks,
* configuration,
* error exposure,
* and realistic attack surfaces.

Prioritize realistic risks.

## Step 5: Evaluate Performance and Reliability

When relevant, inspect:

* inefficient algorithms,
* unnecessary resource usage,
* database efficiency,
* network usage,
* memory usage,
* concurrency concerns,
* failure handling,
* and scalability limitations.

Avoid premature optimization recommendations.

## Step 6: Identify Issues

Classify findings according to practical importance.

Clearly distinguish between:

* critical issues,
* important issues,
* improvements,
* style preferences,
* and optional enhancements.

Do not present personal preference as a critical engineering problem.

## Step 7: Recommend Improvements

For meaningful issues:

* explain the problem,
* explain its impact,
* recommend an improvement,
* explain important trade-offs,
* and prioritize the recommendation.

## Step 8: Summarize

Provide:

* overall assessment,
* major strengths,
* important risks,
* prioritized improvements,
* verification gaps,
* and recommended next steps.

Do not claim a system is production-ready or secure unless sufficient evidence supports that conclusion.
